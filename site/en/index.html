The Derive Notebooks

Observations from a computer-algebra scratchpad

Francesco Messina · October 2026

Read the notePDF, in English Leggi la notaPDF, in Italian Integral editionPDF, in Italian: every expression, with commentary

Between 2009 and 2011 the author used the computer-algebra system Derive as a notebook for mathematical experiments: twenty-three files, 792 expressions. The program is no longer in use, and the files were recovered by decoding their binary format.

The note presents no new theorems. It collects seven themes in which the experiments touch known mathematics, and for each one it says what the notebook saw, what is true, what is already known and what remains open. Every numerical value quoted is checked by a script included in the repository.

Seven themes

  1. The exponential and the half-angle tangent

    Five notebooks contain pieces of one idea: writing the exponential as a rational function of the tangent of the half angle. Followed all the way, it leads to Euler's continued fraction for \(\mathrm{e}\), to Padé approximants and to the bilinear transformation of digital filters.

    \[\begin{gathered}\mathrm{e}^{s}=\frac{1+\tanh(s/2)}{1-\tanh(s/2)}\\[4pt] \frac{\mathrm{e}-1}{\mathrm{e}+1}=[0;\,2,6,10,14,\dots]\end{gathered}\]

  2. A chain of factorials that breaks

    \(1!\,3!\,5!\,7!=10!\), but the chain stops there: its last step is \(6!\,7!=10!\), the only known nontrivial solution of \(a!\,b!=c!\). The note proves that the chain never closes again.

    \[\begin{gathered}1!\,3!\,5!\cdots(2p+1)!=T_{p+1}!\\[2pt] \iff p\le 3\qquad\bigl(T_m=\tfrac{m(m+1)}{2}\bigr)\end{gathered}\]

  3. The mean and the midpoint

    For every solution of \(f''=\mu^2 f\) the ratio between the mean over an interval and the value at its centre depends only on the width: this is the mean value theorem for the Helmholtz equation in one dimension.

    \[\frac{1}{2h}\int_{c-h}^{c+h}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x =f(c)\,\frac{\sinh\mu h}{\mu h}\]

  4. The cardinal sine and its twin

    The function \(((-1)^x-1)/(\pi x)\) is \(\mathrm{i}\) times the analytic signal of the cardinal sine: its “twin” is the Hilbert transform, and unlike the cardinal sine it is not integrable.

    \[\frac{(-1)^x-1}{\pi x} =\mathrm{i}\Bigl(\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}+\mathrm{i}\,H\Bigl[\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}\Bigr]\Bigr)\]

  5. Steps made of logarithms

    The Heaviside step built from logarithms and absolute values is exact, and it is the limiting case of Maslov dequantization: the same pair that neural networks call softplus and ReLU.

    \[\ln\frac{|\mathrm{e}^x-1|+|\mathrm{e}^x+1|}{2}=\max(x,0)\]

  6. Two sequences for \(\mathrm{e}\)

    The notebooks' sequence does not converge faster than the classical one: it has the same error, with the opposite sign. Their mean does.

    \[\begin{aligned}&\tfrac12\Bigl[\bigl(1+\tfrac1n\bigr)^{n}+\bigl(1-\tfrac1n\bigr)^{-n}\Bigr]\\ &\qquad=\mathrm{e}\Bigl(1+\frac{11}{24n^2}+O(n^{-4})\Bigr)\end{aligned}\]

  7. A Dirac delta off the real axis

    Evaluated at a complex point, a family approximating the delta gives an ordinary function; but the result depends on the family chosen. An open question, not a result.

    \[\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_n(z)=\frac{z\,\operatorname{Im}z}{2\mathrm{i}\,|z|^3}\]

The recovery

Derive's .dfw files can no longer be opened by any current program. The repository contains the original files and the code that decodes their binary format, translates the expressions into mathematical notation, checks them numerically and produces the PDFs. It may be useful to anyone who still has Derive notebooks to open.
