Between 2009 and 2011 the author used the computer-algebra system Derive as a
notebook for mathematical experiments: twenty-three files, $792$ expressions.
The program is no longer in use, and the files were recovered by decoding
their binary format. This note presents no new theorems. It collects seven
themes in which the experiments touch known mathematics --- the half-angle
tangent and Euler's continued fraction for $\ee$, the equation $a!\,b!=c!$,
the mean value theorem for the Helmholtz equation, the Hilbert transform,
Maslov dequantization, the acceleration of a sequence for $\ee$, the Dirac
delta off the real axis --- and for each one it says what the notebook saw,
what is true, what is already known and what remains open. It also collects
the formula the notebooks use most often, with its variants and its general
form. For a chain of
equalities between factorials, true in its first four cases, it proves that
the chain never closes again. Every numerical value quoted is checked by a
script included in the repository.
