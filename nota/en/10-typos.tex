\section{Two typos and a trap}
\label{sec:refusi}

Reading the expressions one by one also brings the mistakes to light. Two
typos remain in the notebooks, both in lines typed by hand; a third slip, a
spurious factor in \riga{notch.dfw}{15}, is corrected by the author in the
very next line. Derive's own mistakes are of a different kind: a wrong limit
(Section~\ref{sec:e}) and a numerical answer to a question that has none,
the trap described below.

\paragraph{\riga{teoria.dfw}{13}.}
Lines \righe{teoria.dfw}{8}{13} record, for $x=1/n$ and $m=2,\dots,7$, the
quantity
\[
  \varepsilon\left[(1+x)^m-1-x^m\right]
  =\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\binom{m}{k}x^k ,
\]
that is, the binomial expansion of $(1+x)^m$ without its first and last
terms. This is not the propagation of an error on $x^m$, which would give
$m\,x^{m-1}\varepsilon$; the two agree only for $m=2$. Lines \#8 to \#12
follow the law exactly. Line \#13 instead reads
\[
  7\varepsilon\,\frac{n^5+3n^4+10n^3+5n^2+3n+1}{n^6},
\]
whereas the law, and with it the symmetry of the coefficients
$\binom{7}{k}=7,21,35,35,21,7$, requires $5n^3$ in place of $10n^3$. The
discrepancy from the law is exactly $35\varepsilon/n^3$.

\paragraph{\riga{factor.dfw}{5}.}
The line reads
\[
  1!\,3!\,5!\,7!\,9!-15!=2^{11}\cdot 3^{6}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 7^{3},
\]
but the difference is $9{,}144{,}576{,}000=2^{11}\cdot3^{6}\cdot5^{3}\cdot7^{2}$:
there is one factor of $7$ too many. The other ten factorizations in the same
notebook are exact, both those typed by hand (\righe{factor.dfw}{6}{10}) and
those produced by Derive (\righe{factor.dfw}{11}{15}). What these differences
measure is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:fattoriali}.

\paragraph{The trap: \righe{mate.dfw}{21}{48}.}
The notebook \texttt{mate.dfw} contains some thirty lines that look for a
value of a parameter $k$ such that
\[
  \frac{\cos^2(x+ky)-\cos^2(x-ky)}{\cos(2x+2ky)-\cos(2x-2ky)}=\frac12 .
\]
But the left-hand side equals $\tfrac12$ for \emph{every} $k$ (wherever the
denominator does not vanish), because $\cos^2 A-\cos^2 B=\tfrac12(\cos 2A-\cos
2B)$: the equation is an identity. Derive's numerical solver returns a number
all the same, $k=-4.999999701$, and returns the very same number for
different equations (lines \#23, \#27, \#30, \#33 and \#43): it is not a
solution but the point where the iteration stops. The following lines take it
seriously, divide it by $\pi$ and look for its meaning. They are worth
keeping as a reminder: a numerical solver always answers, even when the
question has no single answer.
