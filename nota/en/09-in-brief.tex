\texttt{Sudoku.dfw} \newline April 2009 &
A valid sudoku grid treated as a matrix: its determinant is
$-2{,}975{,}130$ (\riga{Sudoku.dfw}{2}). The determinants of the nine blocks
and of the rows folded into $3\times3$ matrices show no relation to it. \\
\texttt{teoria.dfw} \newline May 2009 &
\righe{teoria.dfw}{1}{7}: a table that assigns to each operation a
``defect'' $\varepsilon$. The notebook does not say what $\varepsilon$ is;
under a reconstructed reading, a relative error common to all operands
($x\mapsto x(1+\varepsilon)$), sum and difference preserve it, product and
square double it, the $n$-th root divides it by $n$ and the quotient cancels
it exactly: this is the principle of ratiometric measurement.
\righe{teoria.dfw}{14}{19}: perturbing $(x+1)^2=0$ into
$(x+1)^2=2\varepsilon$ separates the roots by $2\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$, because
a double root is sensitive like $\sqrt\varepsilon$.
\righe{teoria.dfw}{20}{27}: the equation $10^{-x(x+1)/2}=1/(10^{x+1}-1)$ has
a root near $2$, $x=1.99971\dots$ (line \#25), because
$10^3=1000\approx999$; the value $x=10$ from the numerical solver (\#27) is
not a solution. Lines \#8--\#13 are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:refusi}. \\
\texttt{j.dfw} \newline July 2009 &
\righe{j.dfw}{3}{4} write $\ii=\lim_{x\to0}(1-\cos x)/\sin x$, but the limit
is $0$: no limit of real quantities gives $\ii$. The correct answer, $\ii$ as
the ratio between the derivative of $(-1)^x$ and $\pi(-1)^x$, comes in
\texttt{cosinc.dfw} (Section~\ref{sec:cosinc}). \\
\texttt{teoria2.dfw} \newline November 2009 &
Curves $y=ax^n$ through the points $(k/2^{n-1},k/2^{n-1})$ of the bisector,
with $a=2^{(n-1)^2}k^{1-n}$ (\righe{teoria2.dfw}{29}{31}). The parabola
$y=2x^2/k$ meets the line $y=-x+k/2$ at $x=k(\sqrt5-1)/4$, a point related to
the golden ratio (\riga{teoria2.dfw}{10}). \\
\texttt{limit.dfw} \newline December 2009 &
A single line: $|x|\le\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\bigl(\frac12+x^2\bigr)$, which follows
from the inequality between arithmetic and geometric means, with equality for
$|x|=1/\sqrt2$. \\
\texttt{sin-cos.dfw} \newline January 2010 &
Hyperbolic functions built on $\ee^{\pi x}$ and $\pi^{\ee x}$. Since
$\ee^{\pi x}/\pi^{\ee x}=\ee^{kx}$ with $k=\pi-\ee\ln\pi=0.029894\dots$, the
whole notebook is made of $\cosh kx$ and $\sinh kx$. The same number
reappears in \righe{e.dfw}{56}{69} as $\ee^{\pi}/\pi^{\ee}=\ee^{k}$, with
$\sinh k=0.029898\dots$ \\
\texttt{notch.dfw} \newline January 2010 &
The design of a band-stop filter: transfer function, limiting cases and the
canonical second-order form, with the antiresonance frequency
$\omega_n=1/\sqrt{lc}$ and the quality factor (\righe{notch.dfw}{15}{20}).
Line \#15 has a spurious factor $r$ in the constant term; the author removes
it in line \#16. \\
\texttt{equazioni.dfw} \newline January 2010 &
Equations of degree one to four solved by setting $z=x+\ii y$ and separating
real and imaginary parts. The imaginary part of $p(x+\ii y)$ is $y$ times a
factor that reduces to $p'(x)$ at $y=0$: this is why, in the quadratic case,
the complex roots lie on the line $x=-b/(2a)$. \\
\texttt{cost\^{}2.dfw} \newline June 2010 &
The functions $\cos t^2$ and $\sin t^2$, whose integrals are the Fresnel
integrals; the integral over the whole line of
$\frac12(\cos\omega t^2+\sin\omega t^2)$ is $\sqrt{\pi/(2\omega)}$
(\righe{cost\^{}2.dfw}{19}{20}). \\
\texttt{e.dfw} \newline August 2010 &
Besides what is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:e}: the parabola with roots
$\ee$ and $\pi$ encloses an area $(\pi-\ee)^3/6$ (\righe{e.dfw}{43}{44}), and
the comparison between $\ee^\pi$ and $\pi^\ee$ goes through the same $k$ as
\texttt{sin-cos.dfw}. \\
\texttt{pigreco.dfw} \newline August 2010 &
Five routes to $\pi$. Among them: $4\,\bigl((1/2)!\bigr)^2=\pi$
(\righe{pigreco.dfw}{20}{21}); the ratio in \riga{pigreco.dfw}{22} equals
$\sgn(x)\,\pi$ for every $x$, because $\cos2a-\cos2b=2(\cos^2a-\cos^2b)$ and
$\arctan x+\arctan(1/x)=\sgn(x)\,\pi/2$; $(\pi^x+\ee^x)^{1/x}$ tends to $\pi$
as $x\to+\infty$ and to $\ee$ as $x\to-\infty$
(\righe{pigreco.dfw}{25}{26}). \\
\texttt{numeri primi.dfw} \newline August 2010 &
The sum of the reciprocals of the first nineteen primorials,
$\frac12+\frac16+\frac1{30}+\dotsb$. The full series equals
$0.7052301717\dots$ and is irrational: if it were $a/b$, then, with $P_k$ the
product of the first $k$ primes, $b\sum_{k>n}P_n/P_k$ would be an integer
strictly between $0$ and $b/(p_{n+1}-1)$, which is impossible for
$p_{n+1}>b+1$. \\
\texttt{fx\^{}gx.dfw} \newline November 2010 &
A function that seems to depend on $x$ and equals $1/\ee$ wherever
$0<\sin x<1$:
$(\sin x)^{1/\ln(1/\sin x)}$, because $u^{1/\ln(1/u)}=\ee^{-1}$ for every $u$
in $(0,1)$. \\
\texttt{fattorazionale.dfw} \newline January 2011 &
$\prod_{n=1}^{k-1}\bigl(\frac1n-\frac1k\bigr)=k^{1-k}$: each factor equals
$(k-n)/(nk)$, and the product of the numerators cancels that of the
denominators $n$. The values for $k=2,\dots,12$ are in lines
\righe{fattorazionale.dfw}{2}{12}. \\
